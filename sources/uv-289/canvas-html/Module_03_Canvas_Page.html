💌 Sprint 2: Dual Focus — Build and Communicate

This sprint demands two things at once.

You're continuing to build MVP features, but you're also preparing to communicate your design needs to people who don't speak code. The design brief is your bridge to GRD-242 — graphic design students who will create visual assets for your project.

Key Focus Areas:

  • Improved estimation: Use Sprint 1 velocity to set a realistic Sprint 2 goal — no more guessing
  • Design brief creation: Translate technical requirements into language designers understand
  • Continued feature delivery: Ship more working features through Sacred Flow
  • Peer review practice: Give and receive feedback on design briefs before they go to GRD-242

Why This Matters: In industry, developers who can communicate effectively with designers ship better products. This is a career skill you'll use in every cross-functional team.

🔬 Hands-On Activity: Design Brief Writing Workshop

What you'll do:

Write a design brief that a GRD-242 student can act on without needing to understand your codebase. This is a professional communication exercise as much as a design exercise.

What You'll Practice:

  • Describing your project's purpose and audience in plain language
  • Specifying design deliverables with dimensions, formats, and use cases
  • Creating mood boards or visual reference collections
  • Writing constraints (accessibility requirements, brand guidelines, technical limitations)
  • Setting realistic timelines for design deliverables

Design Brief Sections:

  1. Project Overview: What is this project and who uses it?
  2. Target Audience: Demographics, technical comfort, usage context
  3. Brand Direction: Mood, tone, color preferences, competitor examples
  4. Specific Deliverables: Exactly what assets you need, with specifications
  5. Timeline & Priority: What you need first and when
  6. Technical Constraints: File formats, sizes, responsive requirements

💡 Andrew's Tips for Success

  • Use your Sprint 1 data honestly: If you completed 12 story points in Sprint 1, plan for 12-15 in Sprint 2 — not 30. Sustainable pace beats heroic sprints every time.
  • Write the design brief for a designer, not a developer: If your brief says "implement responsive CSS grid with 12-column layout," you've failed the assignment. Say "the homepage should look good on phones and laptops" and include screenshots of designs you admire.
  • Peer review is practice for real collaboration: When you review a classmate's design brief, pretend you're the GRD-242 student receiving it. Would you know what to build? If not, say so specifically.
  • Velocity improvement isn't guaranteed: Sprint 2 might actually be slower if you're juggling the design brief. That's okay. Document why, and adjust for Sprint 3.
  • Start the design brief early: Don't wait until Week 5. Draft it during Week 4 so you have time for peer review and revision before Module 4's presentation.

📣 Andrew's Note

This module is where capstone projects start to feel real. You're building software AND communicating with another team. That dual pressure is exactly what professional development looks like. Welcome to the real world.

The design brief is not busywork. In Module 4, you'll present this brief to GRD-242 students who will actually create design assets for your project. If your brief is vague, you'll get vague designs back. If it's specific and professional, you'll get assets you can actually use. The quality of your communication directly determines the quality of what you receive.

I review every design brief before it goes to GRD-242. If yours isn't ready, I'll send it back for revision. Take the peer review seriously — your classmates' feedback will make your brief stronger.
