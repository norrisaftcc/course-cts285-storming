🤝 Design Collaboration: Working Across Teams

This is the module where collaboration gets real.

You've been building software autonomously for three sprints. Now you're working with GRD-242 graphic design students who have different skills, different vocabularies, and different timelines. This is the most realistic professional experience in the entire capstone.

Key Focus Areas:

  • Professional presentation: Present your design brief clearly and persuasively to your GRD-242 partner
  • Communication protocols: Establish how and when you'll communicate (Slack, email, meetings, shared folders)
  • Dependency management: Some stories are now blocked by design assets — learn to manage "waiting on design" states
  • Feedback frameworks: Use SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) to give constructive feedback on design drafts

Why This Matters: No developer works in isolation. Cross-functional collaboration is the norm in every tech company. How you handle communication, dependencies, and feedback defines your professional reputation.

🔬 Hands-On Activity: Feedback Framework Lab (SBI)

What you'll do:

Practice giving professional, actionable feedback using the Situation-Behavior-Impact framework. You'll work with sample design deliverables and craft feedback that a designer can actually use.

What You'll Practice:

  • Separating personal preference from usability feedback
  • Structuring feedback that's specific and actionable
  • Giving positive reinforcement, not just criticism
  • Framing technical constraints without dismissing creative choices
  • Negotiating design decisions with respect for the designer's expertise

Common Feedback Mistakes to Avoid:

  • "I don't like it" (vague, unhelpful)
  • "Make it pop more" (subjective, unactionable)
  • "Can you just make it look like Apple?" (unrealistic, dismissive of original work)
  • "This is wrong" without explaining why or suggesting alternatives

💡 Andrew's Tips for Success

  • Present like a professional, not a student: Your design brief presentation is practice for client-facing meetings. Dress the part. Speak clearly. Have your materials ready. First impressions with your GRD-242 partner set the tone for the entire collaboration.
  • Over-communicate, especially early: The biggest collaboration failures come from silence. If you haven't heard from your design partner in 3 days, reach out. Don't assume "no news is good news."
  • Plan for the "waiting on design" reality: Some of your Sprint 3 stories will be blocked by design assets. Have independent stories ready to work on while you wait. Smart backlog management means never being idle.
  • Use SBI every time you give feedback: "I like it" and "I don't like it" are equally useless. Be specific about what works, what doesn't, and why it matters for your users.
  • Document everything: Every agreement, every deadline, every design revision goes in the collaboration log. When misunderstandings happen (and they will), documentation is your safety net.

📣 Andrew's Note

This is the most important module in the capstone. Cross-team collaboration is the skill that separates good developers from great ones. I've seen talented programmers fail in industry because they couldn't communicate with designers, PMs, or clients. This module gives you practice while the stakes are still academic.

Your GRD-242 partners are real people with real workloads. Treat them with the same professionalism you'd show a colleague at a company. Respond to their messages promptly. Be clear about what you need. Thank them for their work. Respect their creative expertise — they know design better than you, just like you know code better than them.

If collaboration goes sideways — missed deadlines, miscommunication, radio silence — come talk to me immediately. Don't wait until Sprint 4 to tell me Sprint 3 had problems. Part of being a PM is escalating issues early.
