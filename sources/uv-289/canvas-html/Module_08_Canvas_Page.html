This is it. You own everything.

There are no assigned roles in Sprint 8. You are the developer, the QA lead, the Scrum Master, and the product owner. This is intentional — in your career, you will often need to self-organize, prioritize ruthlessly, and deliver under pressure.

Key Responsibilities:

💡 Andrew's Tips for Success

  • Your portfolio tells a story — curate it: Don't dump every artifact from every module. Select the best examples that show your growth. Quality over quantity.
  • The velocity narrative is gold: Show how your Sprint 1 estimates compared to Sprint 7. That improvement curve is evidence of learning that employers love.
  • Practice your demo out loud: Reading a script sounds robotic. Practice until you can narrate naturally. Record yourself. Watch it back. Cringe. Improve. Repeat.
  • Your README is your first impression: When an employer visits your GitHub repo, the README is the first thing they see. Make it professional, clear, and complete.
  • Be honest in your self-assessment: Acknowledging what went wrong is more impressive than pretending everything was perfect. Employers want self-aware developers.
  • This is not the end — it's a beginning: The capstone is your launchpad. The skills you built here — Agile, Git, testing, deployment, AI-assisted development — these are the skills that get you hired.

🌟 Preparing for Your Career

This is the final module of the final course in the CSC program sequence. There is no "next module" — there's your career. Here's how to leverage what you've built:

  • GitHub Portfolio: Your capstone repo should be pinned on your GitHub profile. Clean up the README, add screenshots, and make sure the live URL works.
  • Resume Ready: Add your capstone to your resume. Describe it in terms of impact: "Delivered a full-stack [type] application through 8 Agile sprints, deployed to [platform], with AI-assisted development using Claude Code."
  • Interview Prep: You can now answer questions about Agile, Git workflows, code review, deployment, testing, AI-assisted development, and cross-team collaboration with real examples.
  • PM Skills Transfer: The project management skills you learned — sprint planning, velocity tracking, scope management, retrospectives — apply to every job in tech, not just PM roles.
  • AI-Assisted Development: You know how to work with AI tools responsibly. You have a CLAUDE.md, prompt logs, and evidence of engineering judgment. This is a differentiator in the job market.
  • Keep Building: The best way to stay sharp is to keep building. Start a side project. Contribute to open source. The Sacred Flow works for personal projects too.
