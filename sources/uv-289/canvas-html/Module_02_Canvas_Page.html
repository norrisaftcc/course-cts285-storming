🏃 Sprint 1: Your First Autonomous Sprint

What makes this sprint different from CTS-285?

In CTS-285, sprints were guided exercises. Here, you are running the show. You select your own sprint goal, manage your own backlog, track your own velocity, and deliver working software. There's no safety net — and that's the point.

Key Focus Areas:

  • Autonomy: You decide what to build, how to build it, and when it's done
  • Velocity baseline: Track actual story points completed to calibrate future sprints
  • Design discovery: Identify which parts of your project need professional design assets from GRD-242
  • Sacred Flow discipline: Every feature goes through branch → PR → review → merge

Why This Matters: Sprint 1 velocity becomes your planning baseline for the entire capstone. An honest Sprint 1 prevents catastrophic scope problems later.

🔬 Hands-On Activity: Velocity Estimation Lab

What you'll do:

Before writing a single line of code, you need to estimate how much work you can realistically complete. This lab walks you through establishing your personal velocity baseline.

What You'll Practice:

  • Breaking user stories into estimable chunks (1, 2, 3, 5, 8 point scale)
  • Calculating available capacity (total hours minus meetings, classes, life)
  • Setting a sprint goal that's ambitious but achievable
  • Creating a burndown chart to track daily progress

Pro Tip: Most students overestimate their Sprint 1 velocity by 40-60%. That's normal. The goal isn't perfection — it's establishing an honest baseline you can improve from.

💡 Andrew's Tips for Success

  • Velocity is descriptive, not prescriptive: Don't inflate your numbers to look good. An honest 15 points is more valuable than a fictional 40. You're calibrating, not competing.
  • Ship something working: A half-finished feature is worth zero points. Prioritize completing fewer stories over starting many. "Done" means deployed or demo-ready.
  • Design discovery is strategic: Start identifying what you need from GRD-242 now, even though the formal brief comes in Module 3. The earlier you know your design dependencies, the better your Sprint 2 planning will be.
  • The retrospective is not optional: Write down what went wrong. Write down what you'll change. Sprint 2 should be measurably better than Sprint 1 — and that only happens through honest reflection.
  • Commit early, commit often: Small, frequent commits through Sacred Flow are better than one massive commit at the deadline. Your GitHub history tells the story of your work ethic.

📣 Andrew's Note

Sprint 1 is where the rubber meets the road. You have a plan from Module 1 — now execute it. I'm watching your GitHub repos. I want to see branches, PRs, and merges happening throughout the two weeks, not a panic-push on the last day.

About velocity: Every student I've ever taught overestimates Sprint 1. That's fine. The purpose of Sprint 1 is to learn your actual pace so you can plan realistically for Sprints 2-6. If you complete 3 out of 8 planned stories, that's useful data. If you lie about completing 7, you're only hurting yourself in Sprint 2.

Start thinking about GRD-242 collaboration. In Module 3, you'll be writing a formal design brief. The students who start identifying their design needs now will write much stronger briefs than those who wait.
