## Question 1
**Question**: What is the correct order of the Sacred Flow workflow?
**A)** Branch, Issue, PR, Code, Merge
**B)** Issue, Branch, Code, PR, Review, Merge
**C)** Code, Branch, Issue, PR, Merge
**D)** PR, Issue, Code, Branch, Review, Merge
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Sacred Flow follows Issue, Branch, Code, PR, Review, Merge — every change begins with a tracked issue and ends with a reviewed merge.

---

## Question 2
**Question**: During Sprint Planning, the team selects user stories from the product backlog. What primarily determines which stories are selected?
**A)** Whichever stories the developer finds most interesting
**B)** Priority ranking, sprint goal alignment, and team capacity
**C)** The stories with the lowest story point estimates
**D)** Stories requested most recently by stakeholders
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Sprint planning selects stories based on backlog priority (set during refinement), alignment with the sprint goal, and available capacity based on velocity data.

---

## Question 3
**Question**: Which user story format follows the industry-standard template?
**A)** "Build a login page with username and password fields"
**B)** "As a registered user, I want to reset my password so that I can regain access if I forget it"
**C)** "The system shall authenticate users against the database"
**D)** "Login feature — high priority, 5 points"
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: The standard format is "As a [user type], I want [action] so that [benefit]" — it centers the user's need and the value delivered.

---

## Question 4
**Question**: What is the purpose of acceptance criteria on a user story?
**A)** To assign story points to the story
**B)** To define the specific, testable conditions that must be met for the story to be considered done
**C)** To describe the technical architecture needed
**D)** To prioritize the story relative to others in the backlog
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Acceptance criteria are the measurable conditions that define "done" for a specific story — they make completion objective, not subjective.

---

## Question 5
**Question**: A team's velocity for the last three sprints was 12, 15, and 18 story points. What is the best estimate of their capacity for the next sprint?
**A)** 18 points, since velocity is trending upward
**B)** 15 points, the average of the last three sprints
**C)** 20 points, to push the team to improve
**D)** 12 points, to guarantee everything gets finished
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Average velocity over recent sprints (here, 15 points) is the most reliable capacity estimate — it accounts for variability without over-committing or sandbagging.

---

## Question 6
**Question**: In MoSCoW prioritization, what does the "S" in "Should Have" mean?
**A)** Features that are legally required
**B)** Features that are important but not critical for this release — the project succeeds without them
**C)** Features that stakeholders suggested but haven't been analyzed
**D)** Features scheduled for the second sprint
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: "Should Have" items are important and add significant value, but the MVP can ship without them — they are the first candidates for inclusion if capacity allows.

---

## Question 7
**Question**: What is the primary purpose of a risk register?
**A)** To document every bug found during testing
**B)** To track identified risks, their probability, impact, and planned mitigation strategies
**C)** To list all features that were cut from scope
**D)** To record stakeholder complaints
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: A risk register proactively tracks potential threats to the project along with their likelihood, severity, and mitigation plans — it turns surprises into managed concerns.

---

## Question 8
**Question**: A burndown chart shows a flat line for three consecutive days mid-sprint. What does this indicate?
**A)** The team is ahead of schedule
**B)** No work was completed or no stories were moved to "done" during those days
**C)** The sprint goal was achieved early
**D)** The chart is broken and needs to be regenerated
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: A flat burndown line means remaining work has not decreased — the team may be blocked, working on a large story that hasn't been completed, or not updating the board.

---

## Question 9
**Question**: What is the primary purpose of a Sprint Retrospective?
**A)** To demo completed work to stakeholders
**B)** To plan the next sprint's user stories
**C)** To reflect on the team's process and identify actionable improvements for the next sprint
**D)** To update the product backlog priorities
**Answer**: C
**Explanation**: The retrospective focuses on process improvement — what went well, what didn't, and concrete action items — it is distinct from the sprint review (demo) and sprint planning.

---

## Question 10
**Question**: Which of the following best describes a Definition of Done (DoD)?
**A)** A checklist specific to one user story's acceptance criteria
**B)** A shared standard that every user story must meet before it can be considered complete (e.g., code reviewed, tests passing, documented)
**C)** The product owner's approval of the feature
**D)** The point at which story points are recorded in the velocity chart
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: The DoD is a team-wide quality standard applied to all stories — it is separate from individual acceptance criteria and ensures consistent quality across the entire backlog.

---

## Question 11
**Question**: A developer estimates a story at 3 points but it actually takes 13 points of effort. What is the most professional response?
**A)** Mark it as 3 points anyway since that was the original estimate
**B)** Document the miss in the retrospective, analyze why the estimate was off, and use the data to improve future estimates
**C)** Blame the story writer for unclear requirements
**D)** Split the story retroactively into smaller stories to hide the miss
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Estimation misses are learning opportunities — documenting them honestly and analyzing root causes (unclear requirements, unfamiliar technology, hidden complexity) improves future accuracy.

---

## Question 12
**Question**: In the Sacred Flow, why must every code change begin with a GitHub Issue?
**A)** Because GitHub requires it technically before creating a branch
**B)** To create a traceable record linking every change to a documented reason, enabling project visibility and accountability
**C)** To slow down development so developers think before coding
**D)** Because the instructor needs to approve every change
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Issues create traceability — every line of code can be traced back to a documented need, making the project auditable and decisions transparent for current and future team members.

---

## Question 13
**Question**: What distinguishes a Sprint Review from a Sprint Retrospective?
**A)** The review is for the team only; the retrospective includes stakeholders
**B)** The review inspects the product increment (demo); the retrospective inspects the process
**C)** The review happens weekly; the retrospective happens monthly
**D)** They are the same ceremony with different names
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Sprint Review = "What did we build?" (product inspection with stakeholders). Sprint Retrospective = "How did we work?" (process inspection by the team).

---

## Question 14
**Question**: A "Must Have" item in MoSCoW prioritization means:
**A)** The stakeholder really wants it but could live without it
**B)** The release fails without it — it is non-negotiable for the MVP
**C)** It must be completed in the first sprint
**D)** It has the highest story point value
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: "Must Have" means the project literally cannot ship without this feature — it defines the minimum viable product and is the non-negotiable baseline.

---

## Question 15
**Question**: Your burndown chart shows remaining work increasing mid-sprint. What is the most likely cause?
**A)** The team is working faster than expected
**B)** New stories or scope were added to the sprint after planning
**C)** The sprint end date was moved earlier
**D)** Completed stories were accidentally deleted
**Answer**: B
**Explanation**: A burndown line going up indicates scope creep — work was added mid-sprint, increasing the total remaining effort beyond what was planned.
