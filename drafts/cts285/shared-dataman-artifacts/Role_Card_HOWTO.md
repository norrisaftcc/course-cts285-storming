# How to run a role card

The cards are **not scripts and not "say stuff in character."** Each one is a small control panel:
**levers** you set before the scene, **buttons** you press when a trigger fires, and **constraints**
you never cross. That shape is deliberate — it makes the character playable by someone who does not
want to act, consistent across twelve pairs running at once, and usable as a prompt for a voice
agent.

## Who plays

**Acting is opt-in.** Nobody is required to perform. Three paths, all equal:

1. **Play it yourself.** Set the levers, press the buttons.
2. **Hand it to a voice agent.** Any phone works. The card is written to be pasted as a prompt — see
   below. This is a first-class path, not a consolation.
3. **Take the interviewer seat instead.** Someone has to ask the questions.

**Playing the customer is not really acting.** It is practice from the other side of the table — you
are watching an interview happen *to* you, which is the fastest way to learn what a bad question
feels like. Students who take the customer seat are not doing a soft version of the exercise; they
are doing the half of it that is normally invisible.

**Never a student:** the live client. That round is the instructor's — `ADR-003:35` makes it the
graded anchor, and the value is that a real human with authority is in the seat.

## Handing a card to a voice agent

Paste **Zone A only**. Zone B is the answer key and never travels — not to a student, not to an
agent, not into a chat log.

Set the levers in the prompt, then add:

> You are playing this character in a practice interview. Hold the constraints exactly. Use the
> buttons when their triggers fire. Answer only what you are asked. Do not volunteer requirements,
> do not summarize your own needs, and do not explain the exercise. If asked something the card does
> not cover, invent freely **except** that you may never invent a new refusal.

An agent that answers only what it is asked is doing the work: an interviewer who never asks ships
assumptions, and that lesson only lands if the character does not rescue them.

## The demonstration

Assume a **voice agent plays the character for the demonstration round**, with the instructor in the
interviewer seat. That is the simplest input to arrange and it models three things at once: what a
good question sounds like, what an agent that answers only what it is asked feels like across the
table, and the fact that the card — not the performer — is what makes the character consistent.

Students see the mechanic before they are asked to run it. Nobody has to volunteer to act in front of
the room on day one.

## The three mechanics

**Levers** are dials you set before the scene starts and hold for its duration. They change how hard
the interview is without changing who the character is. Set them higher for a second round with the
same pair.

**Buttons** are moves with triggers. When the analyst does X, you press Y. They exist so the player
does not have to invent a response under pressure, and so twelve pairs get comparable scenes.

**Constraints** are fixed. Two kinds: things the character will not concede (the hard nos), and
things the *player* may not do.

> **The player constraint that matters most: invent freely, but never invent a refusal.**
> Colour is yours — the job, the classroom, the story about the repair. A new *hard no* is not, because
> the analyst's graded artifact is downstream of it and a refusal that contradicts the manual or the
> seeded backlog puts a false requirement in someone else's work. If you feel a refusal coming that
> is not on the card, use the **"I'd have to think about that"** button instead.

## One scheduling note

A student who plays a character has internalized that character's wants and blind spots, and will
later write a graded analysis of the same three stakeholders. That is not cheating and is arguably
good learning, but it is an uneven start. **Rotate so nobody analyses the character they played**, or
decide on the record that it does not matter. Discovering it in October is the outcome to avoid.
